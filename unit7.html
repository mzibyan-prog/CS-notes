1. Ethical Issues Privacy · Accuracy · Reliability · Misuse · Theft · Hacking

▼

Ethics in computing refers to moral principles that guide the use of computers and data. Key ethical issues:

IssueDescriptionExample
PrivacyIndividuals have the right to control their personal dataSocial media sites selling user data to advertisers
AccuracyData must be correct and up to dateIncorrect medical records leading to wrong treatment
ReliabilitySystems should work dependably and consistentlyBanking system crashing during transactions
Misuse of computersUsing computers to harm others or break lawsCyberbullying, spreading malware, hacking
Theft of software/dataCopying or using software/data without permissionPiracy, copyright infringement
HackingGaining unauthorised access to systemsBreaking into a company's database to steal customer data
PlagiarismPresenting someone else's work as your ownCopying code from the internet without attribution
📌 Exam tip: For each issue, be ready to explain why it matters and give a real-world example.

2. Cultural & Social Issues Digital divide · Censorship · Globalisation · AI

▼

Cultural and social issues arise from the impact of technology on people and communities:

IssueDescriptionExample
Digital divideUnequal access to technology based on wealth, location, or educationRural areas lacking internet access vs. urban centres
CensorshipGovernments or organisations blocking access to informationSome countries blocking social media or news websites
GlobalisationTechnology enables global communication and businessCompanies outsourcing work to other countries via the internet
AI and societyAI systems making decisions that affect people's livesAI used in hiring processes potentially reinforcing biases
Social media impactSocial media affecting mental health, relationships, and privacyCyberbullying, addiction to social media, spread of misinformation
📌 Exam tip: Be prepared to discuss both positive and negative impacts of these issues.

3. Environmental Issues E-waste · Energy use · Green computing

▼

Environmental issues in computing include the impact of hardware and energy use:

IssueDescriptionExample
E-wasteElectronic waste from old computers, phones, and other devicesToxic materials (lead, mercury) leaking into landfills
Energy useComputers and data centres consume large amounts of electricityData centres using as much energy as small cities
Green computingDesigning, manufacturing, and using computers in eco-friendly waysUsing energy-efficient hardware, recycling old devices
Carbon footprintThe amount of CO₂ produced by computing activitiesStreaming videos, cloud storage, and cryptocurrency mining
How to reduce environmental impact:
1 Use energy-efficient hardware (look for ENERGY STAR labels)
2 Recycle old electronics properly (e-waste recycling programs)
3 Use cloud services that power data centres with renewable energy
4 Turn off devices when not in use (reduce standby power consumption)
5 Virtualise servers to reduce the number of physical machines needed

LawPurposeExample

5. Open Source vs Proprietary Software Definitions · Advantages · Disadvantages

▼

Open source software has source code that is freely available for anyone to use, modify, and distribute. Proprietary software is owned by an individual or company, and its use is restricted by a licence.

AspectOpen SourceProprietary
Source codePublicly availableKept secret
CostUsually freeUsually paid
ModificationAllowed and encouragedNot allowed
SupportCommunity-basedProfessional support from vendor
ExamplesLinux, Apache, Firefox, LibreOfficeWindows, macOS, Microsoft Office, Adobe Photoshop
Advantages of Open Source:
  • Free to use, modify, and distribute
  • Community collaboration leads to rapid improvements
  • More transparent (security flaws can be spotted by many)
  • Customisable to specific needs
Disadvantages of Open Source:
  • May lack professional support
  • Can be less user-friendly
  • No guarantee of quality or security
Advantages of Proprietary:
  • Professional support and regular updates
  • Often more user-friendly and polished
  • Usually comes with warranties and guarantees
Disadvantages of Proprietary:
  • Can be expensive
  • Users cannot modify the software
  • Vendor lock-in (difficult to switch to other software)

6. Licence Types Single-user · Multi-user · Site · Concurrent · Shareware · Freeware

▼

A software licence is a legal agreement that defines how software can be used. Different types of licences allow different usage:

Licence TypeDescriptionExample
Single-user licenceSoftware can be installed and used by one person on one deviceMost retail software (e.g., Microsoft Office Home)
Multi-user licenceSoftware can be used by a specific number of named usersBusiness software for a team
Site licenceSoftware can be used by anyone at a specific location (e.g., a school or company)Educational software for a school
Concurrent licenceSoftware can be used by a limited number of users simultaneouslyServer software with 10 concurrent users
SharewareSoftware distributed for free trial, but requires payment after a certain period or for full featuresWinZip, some games
FreewareSoftware that is free to use, but the source code is not available and it cannot be modified or redistributedAdobe Acrobat Reader, Skype
Public domainSoftware that is not copyrighted and can be freely used, modified, and distributedSome old software, government-produced software
📌 Exam tip: Know the differences between shareware (trial, then pay) and freeware (free forever, but no source code).
